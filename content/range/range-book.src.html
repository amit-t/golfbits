Pick your level. The plan fits your 45-minute Tuesday bucket of 40 balls. On a short Friday, do the first drill and the 10-ball test (20 to 30 balls).

Each level has one cue, two or three drills and a pass test. Stay on a level until you pass its test in two separate sessions. Skipped a week? Repeat your last passed level's test first.

Six minutes with one club, before the first ball. It loosens the hips, back and wrists the swing loads, so you can swing faster later without tweaking anything.

  1. Arm circles

    10 forward, 10 back. Small to big.

  2. Hip hinge with the club on your spine

    Club touching head, upper back and tailbone. Push hips back, knees soft, keep all 3 contacts. 10 reps.

  3. Posture turns

    Club across your shoulders, in golf posture. Turn back until the club points at the ball, then through. 10 each way, slow.

  4. Lunge and rotate

    Step forward into a lunge, turn your chest over the front knee. 5 per side.

  5. Leg swings

    Hold the club as a stick. 10 front-to-back swings per leg.

  6. Wrist hinges

    Hold the club out in front, hinge it up and down. 10 reps.

  7. First 10 balls: SW half swings at 50%

    These are warm-up balls, not test balls. Brush the mat, find the rhythm.

At home, on non-range days

  • Wall posture turns, 15 a day. Backside touching a wall in golf posture. Turn back and through without your backside leaving the wall. This is the same feel as your coach's bag drill.
  • Gym ball against a wall, if you have one: pin it with your hip and turn without letting it drop. Teaches turn, not slide.

This is what makes you look like you belong. Same steps, every shot, under 20 seconds from the practice swing. Use it for all 10 test balls at the range.

  1. Stand behind the ball

    Pick a small target: a flag, a tree trunk, a yardage board. Not "the fairway".

  2. Pick a spot 1 metre ahead

    A leaf or a dark patch on the line to your target. You aim at this, it's easier than the flag.

  3. One practice swing

    Beside the ball, thinking only your level's cue.

  4. Clubface first, then feet

    Set the face square to your spot. Then feet, knees and shoulders parallel to that line.

  5. One look, then go

    Look at the target once, back to the ball, swing. No second waggle session.

On the course

  • Be ready when it's your turn: glove on, club chosen, yardage from your caddie.
  • Stand still and out of the player's eye line. Stay quiet from their practice swing to impact.
  • One practice swing only. Lost ball search is 3 minutes, then drop and move on.
  • Replace divots and rake bunkers, or let your caddie know you want them done.
  • On the range: stay in your bay and never walk forward onto the hitting area.
